Yesterday I got back from Sardinia, I was there for 3 and a half days to visit my parents and my sister Annalisa and her family. I hadn't seen my parents since Christmas, but my sister and her family it had been two and a half years already.\
It was wonderful.\
This is the best time of year to go to Sardinia — there are still few tourists, the days are long and warm, but not too hot.
I went for a swim at Chia, a place I hold dear because I spent so many memorable summers there. As a child we used to camp freely on the sand dunes — so many memories.\
Swimming in that clear sea was incredibly refreshing.

But now let's get to the interview with Gemma.

![Seaside with blue clear water](../../../../assets/images/post-52/pic-2.jpg "I would have chosen a different photo... anyway.")

---

_1) It will soon be two years since you moved to the Netherlands. When you arrived you didn't yet know where you were going to live, you didn't understand Dutch yet, and everything was new. As of today, are you happy with the choice to come and live here?_

_Gemma:_ In some ways yes, in others no.

---

_2) What do you like about life in the Netherlands?_

_Gemma:_ First of all the fact that we now live in a city. It was great growing up in the countryside, but once I became a teenager it's nicer to be in a city to be independent.

I also like the fact that we are exposed to many cultures — the fact that we used to live in a small village, all sheltered, whereas now we're more exposed to real life.

Another thing I like about the Netherlands is that women are valued more here, also in the workplace, and there isn't as much patriarchy as in Italy. You can already see this at a young age, in schools. Girls don't let themselves be pushed around and know how to stand up for their opinions — they learn this from their own mothers. The figure of the independent woman is the norm.

Another thing I like is the organisation — in schools, in public transport, in events. In schools students are valued a lot and not put down by teachers.

---

_3) What don't you like about the Netherlands._

_Gemma:_ The language sounds really bad. Not that it's difficult, all things considered, but the language itself isn't beautiful — it doesn't sound good. The food, and also the fact that it's hard to find good ingredients in supermarkets. The weather and the temperatures, which also affect your mood a lot.

Some aspects of the Dutch mentality. It's good that people are direct, but sometimes they do it in a way that to me seems rude.

Teenagers have little respect for the people around them and for public spaces.

---

_4) A couple of months ago you went back to Tuscany for the first time, what was it like?_

_Gemma:_ It was strange but familiar. It's surreal how life has gone on there — time has passed, but everything seemed the same as before I left. I still always feel more at home in Tuscany than here.

---

_5) You actually didn't want to move to the Netherlands. Would you have preferred to stay in Montaione?_

_Gemma:_ At that moment yes, I would have preferred it, but not anymore. I miss it, but I know that here I have much more freedom and I have learned many things I needed.

---

_6) Don't you think you would have learned them in Italy too._

_Gemma:_ No, because it's precisely the environment I've been exposed to that taught me these things. For example, the people at the ISK (the international transition school), most of whom came from different cultures and backgrounds, including traumatic ones.

The Netherlands is very liberal and most people have an advanced and open mindset.

I've also seen things out there that in a small village like Montaione I would never have experienced.

From one moment to the next, both Sophia and I found ourselves with an enormous amount of freedom. We went from having to be driven everywhere by car and going to school in the same small village where everyone knows each other, to being in a multicultural city where we ride our bikes or take the bus and can go anywhere.

---

_7) Do you think you will live in the Netherlands forever._

_Gemma:_ No, not at all. If I think about myself, no — but if I had to think about having children here, it wouldn't be so bad in the end. The Netherlands is a good place to have a family and raise children. You can let them live in a fairly protected environment — the Netherlands isn't that dangerous — but at the same time they can be exposed to other cultures and take on responsibility very quickly, I think.

---

_8) How do you find school and what do you think are the differences compared to Italian schools._

_Gemma:_ Here students definitely have their own responsibility — nothing is done through parents. The environment is very equal between students and teachers and I think it's less stressful than Italian school.

---

_8) Are you planning to get a job?_

_Gemma:_ As soon as I turn sixteen I want to go and work where Sophia works. She has already told the restaurant managers and they said yes. I wouldn't want to work in supermarkets, like many do from as young as fourteen, because I saw how disorganised it is when I worked in a supermarket for a few months.

---

_9) Do you prefer the new house or the old one._

_Gemma:_ The house itself is better — I have a huge bedroom and the house is much bigger. However, as a location, I preferred the previous one.
